Key Concepts

  • Subsurface modeling uses quad topology and subdivision surface for smooth, deformable organic shapes
  • Hard surface modeling uses the bevel modifier/tool for static mechanical shapes that don't deform well
  • Rounded edges are essential in CG because real-world edges always catch light via specular highlights
  • Faces are one-sided in computer graphics; the normal direction determines which side renders (backface culling)
  • The bevel modifier's clamp overlap setting prevents destructive over-beveling near close edges
  • Boolean operations (Bool Tool) subtract or add shapes, working best on planar faces and away from edges
  • Every triangle is automatically co-planar, but quads and n-gons can become non-planar, causing shading errors
  • Volumetric lighting is achieved by adding a volume scatter material to a cube to visualize light rays

Key Concepts

  • Modifier order matters: solidify should come before subdivision surface to preserve rounded, goopy shapes
  • The smooth tool reduces vertex angles without adding geometry, unlike subdivision surface which adds polygons
  • Sharp points on sub-D models should be modeled as tiny quads rather than single vertices to control rounding
  • Keep viewport and render subdivision levels equal to avoid inefficient workflow
  • Cozy cabin scenes benefit from smaller room proportions rather than large, empty spaces
  • HDR environment images use equirectangular projection to wrap light and reflections around a scene
  • Volumetric lighting requires a large cube with a Volume Scatter shader and low density setting
  • Compositing glow (bloom) works by blurring bright pixels above a threshold and adding them back onto the image
  • Substance Modeler uses Signed Distance Fields (SDF) allowing loose, non-destructive Boolean combination of shapes without worrying about polygon topology

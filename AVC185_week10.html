Key Concepts

  • A manifold mesh must be fully enclosed with no holes, like a shape that could hold water
  • Normals define face direction; inverted normals (shown in red) must be fixed before printing
  • Triangles are always planar, but quads and n-gons can become non-planar and should be triangulated or cut
  • Apply all modifiers and convert objects to raw mesh before running manifold checks for 3D printing
  • The 3D printing pipeline flows from Blender (STL export) to PrusaSlicer (slicing) to G-code (printer instructions)
  • Overlapping geometry between separate mesh shells (building, ground, water) is fine as long as each piece is individually manifold
  • Infill density and brim settings affect print strength, bed adhesion, and print time
  • Relative scale matters: buildings should be sized proportionally large compared to the base to stay within printer detail limits

Key Concepts

  • Cut seams along the length of cylindrical shapes to avoid UV distortion when unwrapping
  • High-poly sculpted detail is baked onto low-poly meshes to balance visual fidelity with performance
  • Only the low-poly mesh needs to be UV unwrapped in a high-to-low poly baking workflow
  • Layering materials in Substance Painter requires masks to control where effects are applied, not just stacking them
  • Projection type (UV vs triplanar) must match the design intent, especially for complex or wrapped objects
  • Remeshing (Ctrl+R) is necessary after major sculpt changes to redistribute topology evenly for further detail work
  • Substance Painter's Iray renderer and post-processing effects (bloom, glare, depth of field) can produce portfolio-quality beauty renders
  • Emissive channels combined with bloom post-processing can simulate glowing effects like illuminated eyes

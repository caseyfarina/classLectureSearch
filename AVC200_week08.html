Key Concepts

  • Glow effects require emission on materials plus a bloom post-processing pass; glow is fundamentally a blur pass added back with a blend mode
  • 2D sprites need the polygon collider (Unity's confusingly named 2D equivalent of a mesh collider) for non-square shapes, while box colliders suffice for square sprites
  • Cinemachine cameras can be switched by simply enabling/disabling them, enabling smooth cutscene-like transitions controlled by blend settings in the Cinemachine Brain
  • Modular design (separate sprites/layers rather than single flat images) gives far more flexibility for parallax, animation, and reuse during level design
  • Parallax (staggering background elements along the Z-axis) is a classic technique going back to Mario that brings depth and life to 2D worlds, while gameplay elements must stay on Z=0
  • Animation of world elements beyond the player's pickup (clouds, stars, idle creatures) creates a sense of a living world and draws attention to interactables
  • Ground detection for jumping relies on matching ground layers between the character's ground-check collider and the ground object; the Reset option can restore default script settings
  • Pixel art's size/resolution should remain consistent across assets to avoid mismatched pixel scale when combining sprites of different canvas sizes

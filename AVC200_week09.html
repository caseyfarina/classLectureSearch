Key Concepts

  • The VR template supports dual functionality: desktop (mouse/keyboard) and VR headset interaction can both be active simultaneously
  • Teleportation (cast and teleport) is preferred over direct movement in VR to reduce motion sickness risk
  • Unity's Library folder is regenerable and should be deleted before transferring projects to speed up file copying
  • Haptic feedback and physical synchronization (fans, railings, animatronics) dramatically increase perceived realism in VR
  • Perceived object weight in VR can be simulated through movement delay (grab weight) rather than true physics
  • XR Trigger Zone replaces standard Input Trigger Zone to properly send signals through the VR interaction architecture
  • Four reusable design patterns—exploration, assembly, step sequences, and sorting—serve as templates for educational VR experiences
  • VR rendering is more performance-intensive than screen rendering because it renders stereoscopically to two separate displays

Key Concepts

  • Interactivity design should consider not just whether something works, but how differently players actually interact with it versus expectations
  • Secondary animation (hats, ponytails, bells, ears) on existing character parts adds huge visual polish with little extra effort
  • Use existing sprites and assets to their fullest before adding new content—constraints often drive stronger creative solutions
  • Cinemachine camera follow and parallax layering create a sense of a larger, richer game world versus a static single-screen view
  • Gatekeeping mechanics (collectibles, triggers, obstacles) are essential to turn a collection of parts into an actual game level
  • Ground check colliders must be positioned correctly relative to the character collider, and objects must be on the correct ground layer for jump logic to work
  • Post-processing effects like vignette and bloom are stylistic choices that should match the specific game's visual design
  • When packaging a Unity project to share for troubleshooting, zip only necessary folders (not the Library folder) and upload to Google Drive instead of Canvas

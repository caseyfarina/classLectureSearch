Key Concepts

  • Pre-production is about visualizing and pitching an idea as convincingly as possible before implementation
  • Taste, experience, and ideas will matter more than technical button-clicking skills going forward
  • The creative process is nonlinear and full of necessary detours, not a straight path from idea to finished work
  • Oblique strategies encourage going around creative blocks rather than forcing through them
  • Students should reframe their 'meta-game' from passing the class to becoming stronger creative artists
  • Virtual reality environments should be designed intentionally rather than mimicking mundane real-world spaces
  • Substance materials in Unity allow high-fidelity texturing without requiring deep 3D modeling experience
  • Triplanar projection solves texture stretching issues when scaling objects in a 3D environment

Key Concepts

  • Choose the right medium: not every idea is best suited for VR versus video tutorials or flat games
  • Minimize player locomotion in VR to reduce nausea, especially for first-time users
  • Use voice-over narration instead of onscreen dialogue to avoid issues with player viewing direction in VR
  • Keep project scope simple and focused rather than overly ambitious for a single-developer timeline
  • Leverage haptic feedback and particle systems to create immersive, VR-specific interactions
  • Infuse personal artistic style and whimsy into educational content rather than defaulting to dry, realistic depictions
  • Block out complex actions (like hammering or tool use) using simple parenting and key-framed animations rather than full hand-tracking
  • Strong pre-production (storyboarding and style frames) leads to smoother production later

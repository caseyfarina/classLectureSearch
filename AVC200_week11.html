Key Concepts

  • Storyboards should focus on one core interaction pattern before expanding scope, given limited project time
  • Environmental storytelling and cultural/contextual details reinforce the educational value of a VR experience
  • Style frames pre-visualize the final look and feel of a VR scene, informing decisions about 2D vs 3D assets and shading
  • Use Modern Primitive add-on in Blender to retain adjustable topology when blocking out shapes
  • Always use even numbers of subdivisions, and keep quad topology for subdivision surface modifiers to avoid n-gon artifacts
  • Before exporting from Blender: apply scale, recalculate normals, and set proper anchor/origin points
  • Substance Painter workflow: import FBX, auto-unwrap UVs, bake mesh maps, then build materials with fill layers and masks
  • Export both textures (URP Metallic preset) and the UV-unwrapped mesh from Substance Painter to ensure materials align correctly in Unity

Key Concepts

  • Style frames, concept art, previsualization, and photo bashing are conceptually synonymous terms for the same previsualization process
  • Get as close as possible to a pixel-by-pixel representation of the final look before production begins
  • Consistency matters more than raw talent; incremental, regular progress beats sporadic bursts of effort
  • When revising, aim to rebuild and refine ideas rather than making only minor tweaks to the original concept
  • Increase specificity rather than scope when revising a project; focus and polish a small idea rather than half-executing a large one
  • Virtual reality offers freedom from the constraints of physical reality—use this to soften the boundaries of imagination
  • Solid pre-production reduces decision fatigue during production, turning production into straightforward execution
  • Avoid unnecessary complexity (e.g., extra rooms or features) that doesn't add artistic value to the experience

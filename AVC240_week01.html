Key Concepts

  • The virtual camera in Blender mimics real-world camera behavior including focal length and clipping planes
  • Focal length affects both framing and the perceived relationship between objects in space
  • Blender's viewport shading modes (Solid vs Rendered) trade speed for visual accuracy, unlike real-world 'WYSIWYG' photography
  • Light source size directly controls shadow softness: larger lights create softer shadows, smaller lights create sharper shadows
  • Course attendance requires being present in person or live online, not passive time-shifted viewing
  • Weekly project cycles run Monday to Tuesday at 8pm, with presentation videos due Wednesdays
  • Students must remain transparent about any AI tool usage in their coursework
  • The semester progresses from camera fundamentals to directing/storyboarding to a final applied music video project

Key Concepts

  • Focal length determines both framing and the perceived compression or expansion of space
  • 35mm is the traditional Hollywood film standard used as the baseline for digital lens equivalence
  • 50-55mm focal length approximates the natural perspective of the human eye
  • Longer (telephoto) lenses compress space; shorter (wide-angle) lenses expand it
  • Skeuomorphic design mimics real-world objects digitally but isn't always the most efficient approach
  • Save incrementally and pack external resources to protect against file corruption
  • Use Alt+D for instances (linked copies) versus Shift+D for independent duplicates to manage performance
  • HDR sky images provide realistic reflections and lighting information for glossy or reflective materials

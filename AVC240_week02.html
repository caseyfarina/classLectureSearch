Key Concepts

  • The storyboard-to-animatic-to-previs pipeline helps plan shots before committing to finished animation work
  • The dolly zoom (zali) combines opposite camera movement and focal length change to distort perceived space while keeping the subject the same size
  • Virtual cinematography is typically additive (building up shots) while real-world film production is subtractive (cutting down footage)
  • Blender defaults to Bezier (ease in/out) keyframes, but linear keyframes look more cinematic for camera movement
  • Image sequences preserve quality without frame rate baked in, so frame rate must be matched manually when importing elsewhere (e.g., 24fps in Blender vs 30fps default in After Effects)
  • The f-stop is a ratio of aperture size to focal length: a smaller number means a larger opening and shallower depth of field, while a larger number means a smaller opening and deeper focus
  • In real cameras, aperture affects both brightness and depth of field simultaneously, requiring cinematographers to compensate with lighting; virtual cameras let you isolate these effects
  • Focus distance determines what is sharp, while f-stop determines how blurry everything else becomes; HDRs provide both reflections and ambient lighting in a scene

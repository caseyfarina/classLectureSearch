Key Concepts

  • Dolly zoom requires linked keyframes on both camera position and focal length to keep the subject size constant
  • Linear keyframes (or cutting clip edges) prevent unwanted acceleration/deceleration, keeping motion cinematic
  • Aperture f-stop number is inversely related to hole size: higher number means smaller aperture and deeper focus
  • Depth of field interacts with real-world object scale; shallow focus on large objects makes them look miniature (tilt-shift effect)
  • Shutter speed controls motion blur and is not visible in the viewport—must render (F12) to preview results
  • Bokeh shape is determined by the number of blades in the aperture iris, not just its size
  • Lowering render resolution percentage is the fastest way to speed up renders when time is limited
  • Check scene statistics (VRAM, memory, face count) in Blender preferences to avoid overloading the computer with heavy assets

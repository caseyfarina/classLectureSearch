Key Concepts

  • VR headset configuration is per-account and per-computer, requiring re-setup on different machines
  • VR requires stereoscopic rendering, meaning double the computational work compared to flat screens
  • Teleport movement reduces motion sickness compared to joystick-based movement in VR
  • Frame rate drops in VR can cause motion sickness because visuals lag behind head movement
  • VR content authoring makes 3D creation more physical and intuitive compared to mouse-based tools
  • Motion blur can convey speed either from a moving object's POV (background blurred) or a stationary observer's POV (object blurred)
  • Key render settings like motion blur steps, shutter length, and volume resolution significantly affect render quality and time
  • Volumetric lighting combined with spotlights creates visible headlight beams and fog effects

Key Concepts

  • Tilt shift effects rely on shallow depth of field combined with a camera angle that creates varying distances between foreground, subject, and background
  • Focal length and depth of field work together: compressing angles with focal length while blurring foreground/background creates a miniature look
  • The viewport cannot accurately display motion blur because it only shows a single frame in time; motion blur requires rendering across multiple frames
  • Longer shutter speed values let in more light and increase motion blur, communicating faster velocity of moving objects
  • Realize Instances should typically be unchecked in the Array modifier for better performance unless individual instance modification is needed
  • High-resolution textures (not mesh complexity) are usually the main cause of high VRAM usage and potential crashes in Blender
  • Rigid body simulations require setting moving objects to Active and stationary objects to Passive, then increasing solver iterations for smoother results
  • Real-world f-stop and focal length values only behave realistically when scene objects are scaled to real-world proportions

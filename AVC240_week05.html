Key Concepts

  • Mixamo provides separate character models and motion-capture animation clips that can be mixed and matched, then exported as FBX files with mesh, bones, and animation combined.
  • Motion capture data has a keyframe on every frame using forward kinematics (FK); the Decimate tool in the Graph Editor reduces keyframe density to make editing easier.
  • Cyclic animations (Make Cyclic in the Graph Editor) allow looping and repeat in both directions around the original keyframes.
  • Displacement mapping requires actual subdivided geometry to move, unlike bump/normal maps which fake detail without altering geometry.
  • Aspect ratio has both a perceptual basis (matching human peripheral vision, which favors wider formats) and a historical/technological basis driven by camera and display capabilities plus commercial competition.
  • Modern media makers are 'postmodern' in that they can freely choose any aspect ratio as an artistic and historical reference rather than being limited by technology.
  • Delivered video should only have black bars on top/bottom OR left/right—never on all four sides—when fitting a different aspect ratio into a 16:9 frame.
  • Pixel aspect ratio should always remain 1:1 (square pixels); changing it distorts the image, unlike the frame aspect ratio which is an intentional creative choice.

Key Concepts

  • Moving camera shots keep the subject in focus while blurring the background; stationary shots do the opposite
  • Rigid body physics simulations can save time but are harder to control precisely than keyframed animation
  • Blender Kit and Sketchfab assets vary widely in quality; scanned models often have excessive polygons and texture sizes
  • Use the decimate modifier and texture resizing (text ops add-on) to optimize heavy imported assets
  • Cycles renders can be extremely slow at default settings; using GPU rendering with denoising and low sample counts drastically improves speed
  • Blender's play blast is a fast way to preview renders directly from the viewport
  • Parenting cameras and lights to objects, or using constraints like Damped Track, creates dynamic tracking shots
  • Compositing nodes in Blender (bloom, grain, chromatic aberration) can mimic real camera artifacts without heavy render costs

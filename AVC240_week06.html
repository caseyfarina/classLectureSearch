Key Concepts

  • Frame rate is an artistic/stylistic decision, not just a technical one
  • Hand-drawn animation historically used lower FPS (12 fps 'on the twos', 8 fps 'on the threes') to reduce drawing workload
  • An image sequence has no inherent frame rate; it must be interpreted correctly in compositing software
  • When combining multiple frame rates in one composition, the container frame rate must be greater than or equal to the maximum frame rate used
  • Quantization (stepped keyframes) can simulate lower frame rates without changing underlying keyframe data
  • Cultural associations with frame rate (24fps as 'cinematic', 30/60fps as 'soap opera' or 'video game' smoothness) are learned, not inherent
  • Three NPR toon-outline techniques: reverse hull (inverted normals mesh), grease pencil line art, and Fresnel-based shader outlines
  • Animation boil refers to subtle frame-to-frame variation that can convey 'aliveness' or be deliberately removed for efficiency (e.g., Pokemon-style animation)

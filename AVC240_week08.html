Key Concepts

  • Nine elements of composition: point, line, shape, texture, pattern, space, depth, balance, contrast, color, and angle
  • Rule of thirds and golden ratio/Fibonacci sequence provide starting points for framing compositions
  • Blender's camera viewport display offers built-in composition guide overlays, with add-ons providing additional options
  • Avoid placing subjects or their heads directly against the frame edge (tangents); headroom and lead room matter
  • Lines in a shot should point toward the important subject to reinforce focal attention
  • LED volume/virtual production technology shifts work traditionally done in post-production into pre-production
  • Depth is communicated through layering foreground/background elements and pulling focus
  • Assignment: create six Blender renders (from at least four different files) covering all nine composition elements, plus find nine real-world media examples

Key Concepts

  • Plug in laptops and free up at least 25% hard drive space to avoid Blender slowdowns and freezing
  • Use WizTree to quickly visualize and diagnose what is consuming hard drive storage
  • Fresnel shading requires curved surfaces to look correct; use inverted hull or grease pencil for low-poly outlines instead
  • Cryptomatte creates a mask (matte) from selected objects, which can then be isolated with Set Alpha and recombined using Alpha Over
  • Bloom effects require lowering the threshold or increasing emission strength so bright areas actually glow in the composite
  • Never work directly off USB/flash drives in Blender—copy files to a local internal drive to avoid corruption and slow performance
  • Adding per-frame randomness (e.g., via the seed value with #frame) prevents static painterly filters from looking repetitive across animation
  • The new composition assignment requires 9 original renders and 9 found media examples (with title/link) illustrating composition principles, usable in realistic or NPR style

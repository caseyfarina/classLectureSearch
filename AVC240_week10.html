Key Concepts

  • The inverse square law means light intensity falls off exponentially, not linearly, with distance
  • Proper exposure stretches pixel values to use the full zero-to-one range, giving more flexibility in post-processing
  • Low bit depth combined with heavy color correction causes banding/dithering artifacts
  • Lower Cycles samples with denoising enabled drastically reduces render time while maintaining quality
  • Key, fill, and edge (rim) lights form the foundation of classic three-point lighting
  • Area lights produce more realistic soft shadows and specular highlights than point/spot lights, since light comes from a surface rather than a single point
  • Color temperature (Kelvin) controls the orange-to-blue tint of light, mimicking real-world sources like candles, tungsten bulbs, and daylight
  • Enclosing a scene in a room enables realistic light bounce (global illumination) via path tracing

Key Concepts

  • The inverse square law means light intensity drops off non-linearly, losing 75% when distance doubles
  • Color temperature in Kelvin: lower numbers appear orange/warm, higher numbers appear blue/cool, with 6500K as neutral white
  • Hard light comes from small sources and creates defined shadows and specular highlights; soft light comes from large sources and creates diffuse, flattering illumination
  • High-key lighting evenly illuminates a scene with minimal shadows for a positive mood, while low-key lighting uses strong contrast for a darker, moodier tone
  • Chiaroscuro is a specific subset of low-key lighting used to emphasize depth and texture
  • Gobos (or cookies) are black-and-white images placed in a light's emission input to cast patterned shadows, like window blinds or the Bat-signal
  • Volumetric scatter with noise textures can simulate atmospheric smoke or haze within a scene
  • Compositing techniques like cryptomatte, mist passes, color ramps, and vignettes can further shape mood, separating foreground and background adjustments
